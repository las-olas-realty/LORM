Las Olas Realty and Management LLC sources off-market multifamily and workforce housing investment opportunities across the Sunbelt, including Florida, Texas, Georgia, and California, and provides asset management, underwriting and due diligence consulting, and owner's representative services to the investment groups that acquire them.

The Sunbelt continues to lead the country in population and job growth, and Atlanta sits at the center of that migration. Multifamily demand across the region remains structurally strong even as national supply lags: NMHC projects the U.S. needs 4.3 million more apartments by 2035 to keep pace with rental demand, and the country lost 4.7 million apartments renting below $1,000 a month between 2015 and 2020. Workforce housing, the segment this asset sits squarely inside, is the tightest part of that shortage. Well-located, well-priced rental housing in growth submarkets is becoming harder to find and harder to replace.

The Opportunity

An off-market opportunity to acquire a 400 to 500 unit workforce housing community in a well-established, transit-served submarket of Southwest Atlanta and Fulton County, Georgia.

An Attractive Basis as Construction Costs Rise

New garden-style and workforce multifamily construction in metro Atlanta is currently running $130,000 to $185,000 or more per unit in hard construction cost alone, before land, entitlements, impact fees, and the 18 to 24 months it now takes to permit and deliver. Materials, labor, and financing costs have continued to push that number higher, with no indication the trend reverses.

This opportunity is priced meaningfully below that replacement cost floor, on an existing, cash flowing asset. For the right group, that is a repositioning opportunity at an attractive basis.

Why the Location Matters

The submarket sits inside a corridor receiving a nine figure public transit investment (dedicated bus rapid transit, streetscape and pedestrian upgrades) now under construction, alongside a major mixed use redevelopment on a nearby former federal site anchored by film and entertainment production. Fulton County's broader employment base, approaching a million jobs across logistics, healthcare, aerospace, technology, and entertainment, continues to support durable demand for attainable, workforce priced rental housing that sits below core market rents.

The Play

In-place rents sit meaningfully below stabilized submarket comparables, with a clear runway to capture that gap through renovation, tightened operations, and hands-on management. This is a value-add, repositioning opportunity for a sponsor with the platform and discipline to execute.
